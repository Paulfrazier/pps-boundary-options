Construction on Portland’s I-5 project running on time, despite forecasted rain

Add Us On GoogleAdd as a preferred source on Google

PORTLAND Ore. (KPTV) - It’s been nearly two weeks since I-5 southbound closed in Portland’s Rose Quarter. On Thursday, The Oregon Department of Transportation (ODOT) said despite a chance of rain this weekend, the highway is still expected to reopen on time.

The estimated five-week-long Rose Quarter Improvement Project is still running on schedule, according to Rose Geber, an ODOT spokesperson.

“We do hope to get the highway open as soon as possible,” Gerber said.

A two-mile stretch of I-5 southbound remains closed. According to the Portland Bureau of Transportation (PBOT), it has seen a 30% rise of detour traffic through a handful of city streets adjacent to the closure. However, the agency said the traffic still isn’t as bad as it anticipated.

“We’re not seeing the worst consequences and impacts that we had been bracing for,” said Dylan Rivera, a PBOT spokesperson.

According to ODOT, the impacted section of I-5 hasn’t been replaced since 1985 and is well past its 25-year lifespan.

On Thursday, construction crews poured their first batch of concrete. According to Project Manager Kenn Hallquist, drivers can expect a much smoother and safer ride once the project is complete.

“That bump you feel when you’re driving down a bridge, it’s going to be lessened on this because the joints were in bad shape,” Hallquist said. “In addition, it’s going to be a little bit wider. It’s going to have a new barrier on the southbound side. The barrier will be brought up to standards now.”

Now that crews have started to pour concrete, they will have to take the weather into consideration.

“Any type of precipitation, any type of rain, it will damage that,” Hallquist said. “...It’ll wet the concrete up and it will actually carry away the cement.”

But despite the rain in the forecast, Hallquist also confirmed the project is still running on time.

I-5 southbound is expected to reopen in about three weeks. But next summer, I-5 northbound will experience a similar closure, according to ODOT, and Hallquist said his crew is already getting prepared.

“Every salaried individual has a notebook out there and we’re taking notes on lessons learned,” he said. “And we’re going to implement those to next year so we can get ahead of the game.”
