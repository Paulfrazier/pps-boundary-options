With a freeway expansion coming, what's the future for Harriet Tubman Middle School?

by Keaton Thomas, KATU StaffMon, May 10, 2021 at 6:23 PM

Portland Public Schools spent more than $10 million to install a state-of-the-art air filtration system at Harriet Tubman Middle School, because the school sits next to Interstate 5. (KATU)

PORTLAND, Ore. — Harriet Tubman Middle School sits just a few dozen feet from Interstate 5. As Oregon plans to widen the freeway, potentially moving it even closer to the school, should the middle school stay in its current location?

Officials with Portland Public Schools are considering their options, one of which could include relocating the middle school. But who would pay for that remains a question.

Students returned to Harriet Tubman Middle School in 2018. Before that, PPS spent more than $10 million on a state-of-the-art air filtration system to ensure the air inside was safe. That's because the air quality outside Harriet Tubman is "negatively impacted" by the freeway, according to a Portland State University study.

"There is no escaping the freeway at Harriet Tubman Middle School," said Malina Yuen, who spent two years attending the school.

On some days, Yuen said classmates with asthma were afraid to go outside.

"You walk outside to recess and you see the field and directly next to it you see a freeway," said Adah Crandall, who also spent two years at Harriet Tubman.

The Oregon Department of Transportation continues to move forward with plans to expand I-5 through the Rose Quarter. One such plan would actually move the freeway 26 feet closer to the school, drawing criticism from opponents. ODOT is also developing another design that would expand the freeway to the west instead.

The question of Harriet Tubman's future, though, is uncertain. One option, depending on what happens with the project, could be relocating the middle school.

"That is a conversation we’re going to have with the larger community because we need to talk to the families and the students and the greater Albina community about what their vision is for this school," said Julia Brim-Edwards, a PPS school board member who also serves on the Executive Steering Committee for the I-5 Rose Quarter Improvement Project.

Brim-Edwards said they are preparing to have those conversations within the coming months. She said the district is also looking at what it costs. For comparison, Brim-Edwards said it costs $65 million to build Kellogg Middle School in Southeast Portland. Relocating Harriet Tubman would likely cost more, according to board members.

So, who pays? Brim-Edwards doesn't think it should be PPS.

"Certainly, I would view that as a state obligation, because they created an environment where it’s not an appropriate place to have a school," said Brim-Edwards, who also pointed out there was a school at that location before I-5 was built.

KATU News went to Megan Channel, Rose Quarter Project Director for ODOT, to ask if ODOT would, or could, pay.

"The short answer is no. ODOT alone cannot relocate the school, and again, it is relative to the impact that the project has on the school. What we know through our analysis and our environmental peer review is that the project does not have a negative impact on Harriet Tubman Middle School and the project will improve air quality in the vicinity of the school," said Channel.

"But that’s like saying it’s bad now, and it will be slightly less bad, which is not the standard that I want for my own children, or anybody else’s children," said Brim-Edwards.

Critics question ODOT's finding that air quality would improve.

The freeway project is still in its initial stages, meaning PPS doesn't have to decide the school's future quite yet. Meanwhile, the future of Harriet Tubman hangs in the balance.
