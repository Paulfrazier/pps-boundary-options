A family guide to the proposed school changes
Every school is a community. A school change can affect friendships, routines, programs, and a family’s 
sense of belonging. This guide explains what is proposed and why.
Scenarios  A and B are proposals, not final decisions. “No changes proposed” means these options do not 
include a change for that school.
Each school has a snapshot and a box explaining the reasons for the proposal. The next page shows 
enrollment estimates, programs, student groups, and maps. Estimates cover 2027-28 through 2031-32. 
They are not current student counts.
The possible benefits are goals. A larger school does not automatically get a full-time librarian, certain 
staff, or specific programs. Those details depend on the district’s staffing rules, budget, and plans for 
making the changes.
A school or program move does not determine where an individual student receiving special education 
services will go. Families and school teams would need to plan each student’s services and support.
The school details, numbers, and maps come from PPS school profiles. This guide explains that information 
in simpler language.

Words you may see in this guide
No changes
What is expected if the district keeps the current schools and programs.
Option A / Option B
The two scenarios. “A and B” means the same information applies to both.
School boundary
The area used to assign students to a neighborhood school based on where they live.
School communities joining
Students from two or more schools would come together. The proposal says which school building would close.
Programs joining
A program would come together at one school or move to another school.
DLI
Dual Language Immersion: learning in English and another language.
School size goal
More than 360 students for K-5 schools; more than 500 for K-8 or middle schools; more than 1,100 for high 
schools. These are planning goals, not ratings of school quality.
Space use goal
The district aims to use 70%-100% of a school’s available room for students. A “No” can mean the school is 
below or above that range.
Title I
Federal money that supports schools serving students from low-income households. The guide shows the 
expected designation.
IEP
An Individualized Education Program: a written plan for a student’s special education services and support.
Choice program
A program with a special focus, such as arts or science. It may serve families beyond a neighborhood school 
boundary.
Student groups
Groups can overlap. Do not add the percentages together. 
*You may notice slight differences in student numbers and/or demographics in schools that don't 
experience change because a focus option school has changed and we assume that students return to 
their neighborhood school.

BEAUMONT
School proposal | Options A and B
This is a proposal, not a final decision.
What is being proposed?
Beaumont would remain open as a neighborhood
middle school (grades 6-8).
Beaumont would welcome 6th-8th grade students
from Beverly Cleary, including students from the
former Irvington boundary.
The Spanish Dual Language Immersion (DLI)
program at Beaumont would move to Roseway
Heights Middle School.
What could students gain?
Full middle school options: Middle school students
gain access to broader electives, athletics, middle
school sports, and learning options.
Concentrated DLI program: Moving Spanish DLI to
Roseway Heights builds a strong, focused language
program location where teachers can plan and work
together.
What else was considered?
Direct neighborhood access: Beaumont provides
nearby building space that allows middle grade
students to stay in their local community.
WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?
Beverly Cleary is changing to K-5: Beverly Cleary is changing from a K-8 to a K-5 model, so middle grade
students need a comprehensive middle school location.
Avoids temporary building moves: Welcoming Beverly Cleary middle schoolers to Beaumont keeps students
close to home and prevents them from having to move to temporary buildings during Beverly Cleary's
planned summer 2027 seismic construction.
Logical school pathways: Adding the former Irvington boundary creates clear, connected middle school
pathways for all students in the area.
Bringing DLI programs together: Moving the Spanish DLI from Beaumont to Roseway Heights builds a middle
school serving the area for the Spanish DLI program that then moves to McDaniel for High School.
