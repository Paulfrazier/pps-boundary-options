7/6/26 Consent 
Agenda 
Resource Synergy Contract is $136,393.20 for waste 
sensors. Have we ever done a look-back to see if this 
saves money? The company website indicates that it can 
save a district 10-15%, which made me wonder, how much 
has it saved the district since the sensors were placed? 
Resource Synergy has identified approximately 
$116,500 in overbilling (savings from Q1 2022 through 
Q1 2024) that the District has actively worked with our 
waste haulers to refund to PPS in credits. 
 
Additionally, the benefit is that PPS, as an 
organization, is reducing our carbon footprint by only 
having our haulers address pick-ups when they are 
necessary rather than emptying empty containers. 
 
We continue to work with Resource Synergy on 
savings and right-sizing our waste containers for 
efficiency. 
7/6/26 Consent 
Agenda 
Multco PFA: This is a great program and revenue stream 
for the District. It’s a fantastic way to introduce 
neighborhood families to their schools before kindergarten. 
Have there been any conversations about expanding this to 
all PPS elementary schools? 
We agree that Preschool for All is a great revenue 
stream and partnership. Since PFA's inception, PPS 
has integrated an increasing number of PFA seats 
into our braided early learners' program each year. In 
this coming school year 2026-27, 273 of our 901 PPS 
PreK seats will be funded by PFA. We also welcome 
priority partners who are funded by PFA into our 
space to increase capacity (e.g. CAIRO, MECP, 
Albina Early Head Start, etc). 
 
While the goal of adding PFA classrooms to every 
PPS elementary school is unlikely - as that would not 
necessarily align with the vision of a mixed delivery 
model of PFA - we will continue to strategically 
expand our partnership with PFA as district funding, 
capacity, enrollment, and community needs allow.

Functional capacity is a practical estimate of the number of students a 
school site can accommodate. 
 
PPS calculates it as a model based on the capacity of each classroom 
which is determined by its size in square feet and the approximate 
percentage of a school day that the instructional spaces are used. The 
utilization rate identifies how much of the gross capacity is being used. 
Most schools do not use 100 percent of the available student capacity in 
the building every period of every day. 
 
Gross capacity is based on the capacity of each classroom ( Classrooms 
less than 800 SF have a student capacity of 24; classrooms greater than 
800 SF and less than 1,000 SF have a student capacity of 27; 
classrooms greater than 1,000 SF have a student capacity of 30.). 
PPS defines Efficient Utilization as a school operating between 70% and 
100% of its Functional Capacity. Schools operating within this range 
generally align student enrollment with available physical footprint. This 
baseline generally supports the education program while maintaining 
room flexibility for dedicated health services, early childhood programs, 
and community partnerships. 
 
K-5 and K-8 schools have a base utilization rate set to 100%, then

reductions for: 
 
Title I 
TSI or CSI 
neighborhood K-8 (not a focus option) 
Co-located neighborhood and immersion programs
