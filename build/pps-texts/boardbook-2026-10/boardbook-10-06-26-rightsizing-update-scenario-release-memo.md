Board of Education 
From:​ Dr. Kimberlee Armstrong, Superintendent 
Dr. Jon Franco, Senior Chief of Operations

Rightsizing Update:  Scenario Release 
Date:​ October 5, 2026 
 
Introduction 
This memo presents two proposed rightsizing scenarios for Board review and community engagement. Both 
scenarios seek to stabilize enrollment, strengthen academic program pathways, and align the district’s use of 
school facilities with student needs. They combine school consolidations, grade reconfigurations, program 
moves, and boundary changes.  
Scenario A proposes 14 school closures, while Scenario B proposes 11. The primary differences concern 
Stephenson, Rose City Park, and Lewis, which would close under Scenario A and remain open under Scenario 
B. Community feedback will inform the Superintendent’s recommendation in mid-November. The Board of 
Education will vote on the Superintendent’s recommendation at a public meeting in December. 
Background 
On April 14, 2026, the Board adopted Resolution 7296, directing Superintendent Armstrong to begin an 
enrollment balancing review and launch a districtwide rightsizing process within Growing Great Schools. The 
direction includes consideration of school and program consolidations, boundary adjustments, special 
program changes, and grade reconfigurations to support well-resourced, sustainable schools and equitable 
access to opportunities for students across the district. 
Following community engagement throughout March and April, the Board adopted four guiding principles and 
an overview of a workplan through Resolution 7307 on May 26, 2026. These principles and applicable Board 
policies have informed the development of the proposed scenarios. 
Central office teams in Academics, Activities and Athletics, Data and Modeling, Operations, and 
Communications and Engagement have worked together to develop two scenarios for feedback from 
community members, families, students, and staff. Both scenarios will be compared with projections of 
enrollment and school sustainability if no action is taken. 
District leaders and planning staff developed the proposed changes using criteria focused on enrollment 
sufficient to sustain stable, adequately resourced programming, effective use of buildings, and keeping school 
communities together through change as much as possible. These criteria sometimes pull in different 
directions: preserving a community may require a larger boundary adjustment, while consolidating an 
underenrolled immersion program may lengthen some students’ trips. Criteria were weighed against one 
another rather than applied in a fixed ranking, with the balance differing by regional conditions.

The scenarios differ primarily in the number of proposed school closures. Scenario B would affect fewer 
families and change fewer boundaries, while leaving 43% of schools below the enrollment needed for stable, 
sustainable programming. Scenario A would involve more transitions and boundary changes while bringing 
67% of schools to sustainable enrollment levels. Community review will help inform how these benefits and 
burdens are balanced in the Superintendent’s recommendation. 
Rightsizing modeling responds to uneven enrollment across schools and programs, underenrolled dual 
language immersion classrooms, and the need to strengthen middle school enrollment. The analysis considers 
functional capacity, facility conditions and planned improvements, geographic access, and existing feeder 
patterns. These conditions differ across regions and require different combinations of changes. 
In the Roosevelt and Jefferson region, dual language immersion consolidation and middle school enrollment 
were primary drivers. In the Grant and McDaniel and Cleveland and Franklin regions, modeling emphasized K–8 
reconfiguration, language pathways, facility considerations, and reducing split feeders. In the Lincoln and Wells 
region, geographic isolation, travel distances, and elementary enrollment were central considerations; 
immersion programs were already consolidated. 
The regional approach also seeks to address lessons from prior southeast changes by considering more 
comprehensive school and program changes, preserving community connections where possible, and limiting 
repeated transitions. The scenarios identify proposed assignments and program locations; receiving school 
readiness, transportation, staffing, and transition supports will require further implementation planning. 
Scenario A  
Overview 
Scenario A proposes 14 school closures, eight grade reconfigurations, 13 program moves, one program 
discontinuation, and 38 boundary changes. Seven K–8 schools would become K–5 schools: Beverly Cleary, 
Laurelhurst, Sunnyside, Bridger Creative Science, César Chávez, Faubion, and Vernon. Metropolitan Learning 
Center would become K–8. 
Program moves consolidate Spanish and Chinese immersion pathways, relocate Creston’s Deaf and Hard of 
Hearing program to Glencoe, move Rose City Park’s Vietnamese immersion program to Vestal, and relocate 
Odyssey to Metropolitan Learning Center. Sunnyside’s environmental focus option would be discontinued; it 
would become a neighborhood school with no new lottery transfers, while existing lottery students could 
remain through fifth grade. Odyssey’s relocation would leave East Sylvan vacant and available for consideration 
through the building repurposing process. No alternative or focus option school closure is proposed.

percent to Hayhurst and 30 percent to Rieke. Stephenson would consolidate into Markham and Capitol Hill 
using the same proportions. Targeted boundary adjustments would balance nearby campuses, shift the 
Maplewood middle school area from Jackson to Robert Gray, and change Skyline’s high school assignment 
from Lincoln to Roosevelt. Skyline would remain K–8. Bridlemile’s existing pathways would be retained 
because changes would substantially affect middle school enrollment.

Scenario B  
Overview 
Scenario B proposes 11 school closures, eight grade reconfigurations, 12 program moves, one program 
discontinuation, and 34 boundary changes. It retains Stephenson, Rose City Park, and Lewis as open schools. 
Stephenson and Rose City Park would have no proposed changes; Lewis would receive part of Whitman’s 
boundary. Vietnamese immersion would remain at Rose City Park. 
The eight grade reconfigurations, other program moves, Sunnyside focus option discontinuation, and Odyssey 
relocation described in Scenario A also apply to Scenario B. The two scenarios therefore share most of their 
proposed structural changes, while presenting different approaches to elementary consolidation in three 
regions.

Moving Forward and Next Steps 
Community engagement will provide students, families, staff, and community members opportunities to review 
both scenarios, ask questions, and share concerns and priorities. Feedback will help identify the effects of 
proposed changes on school communities, access to programs, transportation, and the supports needed for 
successful transitions. 
The district will consider community input alongside the regional analysis, equity impact findings, enrollment 
and capacity modeling, and receiving school readiness work. This review will inform refinements to the 
scenarios and the identification of implementation needs and mitigation strategies. 
The Superintendent will incorporate community engagement into a recommendation to the Board in 
mid-November. The recommendation will describe the proposed changes and how community input informed 
them, along with implementation considerations and transition supports for further Board review.
