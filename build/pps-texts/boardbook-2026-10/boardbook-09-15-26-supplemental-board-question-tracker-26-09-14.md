7/6/26 Consent Agenda Resource Synergy Contract is $136,393.20 for waste sensors. Have
we ever done a look-back to see if this saves money? The company
website indicates that it can save a district 10-15%, which made me
wonder, how much has it saved the district since the sensors were
placed?
Resource Synergy has identified approximately $116,500 in
overbilling (savings from Q1 2022 through Q1 2024) that the
District has actively worked with our waste haulers to refund
to PPS in credits.
Additionally, the benefit is that PPS, as an organization, is
reducing our carbon footprint by only having our haulers
address pick-ups when they are necessary rather than
emptying empty containers.
We continue to work with Resource Synergy on savings and
right-sizing our waste containers for efficiency.
7/6/26 Consent Agenda Multco PFA: This is a great program and revenue stream for the
District. It’s a fantastic way to introduce neighborhood families to
their schools before kindergarten. Have there been any
conversations about expanding this to all PPS elementary schools?
We agree that Preschool for All is a great revenue stream and
partnership. Since PFA's inception, PPS has integrated an
increasing number of PFA seats into our braided early
learners' program each year. In this coming school year
2026-27, 273 of our 901 PPS PreK seats will be funded by
PFA. We also welcome priority partners who are funded by
PFA into our space to increase capacity (e.g. CAIRO, MECP,
Albina Early Head Start, etc).
While the goal of adding PFA classrooms to every PPS
elementary school is unlikely - as that would not necessarily
align with the vision of a mixed delivery model of PFA - we will
continue to strategically expand our partnership with PFA as
district funding, capacity, enrollment, and community needs
allow.
8/3/26 Consent Agenda PS98109, how is this different than the contract we approved in
June for temporary staffing for IT services, and why do we need
another $600,000?
This was answered in the board meeting
8/5/26 Rightsizing
Could you please ask Dr. Greene (or Margaret or Jon) for the data
referenced by Dr. Greene last night when I asked about families
leaving PPS during / post-SEGC
The information referenced can be found on pages 48-50 in
Domain 2.  This secttion reviews the dual language immersion
programs specifically.  The information looks not only at
enrollment in the program after it was relocated but also at
enrollment in the district as well.  p. 49 shows the summmary
graph of the percentage of students leaving the district
comparing students enrolled in DLI programs and those not
enrolled in DLI.  "Taken together, these findings suggest that
SEGC-related DLI program relocations did not appear to
result in widespread district exit among DLI students." (p.49).

8/3/26 Consent Agenda Re: DR 98148, why are we doing social emotional learning online?
(the irony of this is not lost on me)
Could you please explain whether this is a new contract or a
renewed contract? And if new, why are we doing this online?
The social emotional curricular tool, We Do it for the Culture,
is not an online program. We purchased digital licenses for
this program, but it is for teacher resources only. This program
integrates multimedia into their curriculum, but students are
not working independently (or in groups) on their computers.
All of the SEL curricula are hands-on and interactive; none of
the three programs are virtual programs.
Rightsizing
Could you please provide data on the number of kids vs.
enrollment? Are the number of kids truly declining or just enrollment
at PPS because parents are making other choices because of
unsafe/large classrooms?
Rightsizing
Our reserach does not support the premise that parents are
not choosing PPS due to "large/unsafe" classrooms. Here are
the results of our study about why families leave:
PPS-Why They Stay and Leave - Summary Report - Ja…
Regarding enrollment, PPS enrollment has been declining
since the 18-19 school year. Several factors are impacting our
enrollment: Catchment percentages are lower, birth rates
continue to decline, and fewer families are moving into
Portland since the pandemic and those families are not having
children or bringing school-aged children to Portland.
Neighborhood catchment rates are published on our website:
https://www.pps.net/fs/resource-manager/view/d65fc69c-e9b5
-4c13-8199-545d12deb541
We have created a public dashboard and within it, developed
an OSAS/Enrollment tab to see the relationship between
school size and enrollment. At this time, no relationship is
noted. Here is the link to the dashboard:
https://www.pps.net/departments/dataaccountability/data-and-
accountability/data-strategy-and-insights/academic-portal
As we begin our right-sizing conversation, could you please provide
me with the testing data (math, ELA, etc.) for the schools that are
under 300, over the last few years (maybe since the year before
COVID since that is when this downward enrollment trend gained
speed).

The Grice-Adair draft findings CMGC memo states “Project budget
is expected to be in the range of $60M with Summer 2027 targeted
as the approximate start date for project related construction.”
The total budget for Grice-Adair is $60M inclusive of the building
purchase of $16M, leaving $44M for the project budget. Can staff
please revise/clarify the memo for Boardbook?

project, not just the construction budget. Total project budget
is $60M, minus purchase of property for $16M, leaving $44M
for renovations. We estimate the total construction cost to be
in the Range of $35-40M.
(1) what is the estimated cost savings to closing a school?

realize there is nuance to this as not all positions at a closed school
will be eliminated, and some positions may move to a new location.
So I guess I am asking for the general estimated loss, taking the
nuance into consideration.
