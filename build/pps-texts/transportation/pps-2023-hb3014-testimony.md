# PPS Superintendent letter to House Education Committee re HB 3014 (Feb 22, 2023)

Unofficial text extraction for research. May contain extraction errors; consult the original at https://apps.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2023R1/Downloads/PublicTestimonyDocument/53708.

PORTLAND PUBLIC SCHOOLS
                                                                  Government Relations

February 22, 2023

Chair Neron, Vice-Chairs Hudson and Wright, and members of the House Education Committee:

As Superintendent of Oregon’s largest district, Portland Public Schools (PPS), I write to lend our
support for HB 3014 and to advocate for additional resources for school districts to provide
multimodal forms of transportation.

PPS supports the aims of this legislation to create opportunities for students to walk, bike, or
roll to school safely. These modes of active transportation connect our community, encourage
youth to engage in fun and healthy activities, and reduce greenhouse gas emissions.

It has been a joy for PPS, and for our city, to witness the excitement and sense of community at
the heart of the Alameda bike bus. We commend Coach Balto and the team of teachers,
parents, volunteers, who have shown up to get students safely to school in a fun and active
environment.

At PPS, we work with a diverse community of parents, students, and organizations to advocate
for and promote the use of active and shared transportation to and from schools throughout
the Portland area. Our partnership with Tri-Met allows students to access public transit services
throughout the school year while acquiring lifetime transit ridership skills. Through our work
with PBOT, we are improving local infrastructure to ensure students have safe routes to school.

While we support the aims of this policy, we remain bound by our statutory requirements to
provide transportation for elementary students who reside more than one mile from school and
for secondary school students who reside more than 1.5 miles as well as students on
supplemental plans. It is important that school districts like ours have enough resources to
provide bus services, safe transportation services for students with disabilities, and supports for
students who walk or bike to school.

As the state considers expanding opportunities for students to use active transportation to get
to school, PPS urges the Legislature to explore more ways to fund this critical initiative.
Removing the limitation on reimbursement funds for applicable student transportation options
