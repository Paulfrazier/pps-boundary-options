Who these kids are, and how the maps fix them

Under the recommendation, 1,216 K–5 kids are assigned more than a mile from home while an open school sits within a mile. On average they walk 1.34 mi to an open school about 0.75 mi away. Nearly all live in a few clusters, and most clusters come from one of two causes. Either a closing school's whole area goes to one receiving school, or a school's line leaves a corner of its area far from the building.

The fix is almost always the school that is already closer. In both maps most of these kids go to the nearby school, and their average walk falls from 1.34 mi to about 0.8 mi. About 110 of the 1,216 are still flagged in Map A, and about 140 in Map B. Redrawing lines also creates a few new cases (about 110 in each map). Counting those, the total drops from 1,216 to 226 (Map A) and 248 (Map B). The worst case, over a mile with a school within half a mile, drops from 115 kids to 1 (Map A) and 0 (Map B).

What it costs. Some kids whose school stays open move to a different one. That is how a nearby school makes room, or how a school that is too small gets big enough. The maps allow those moves only when they shorten the walk or fill a short school, not to shave off a few hundredths of a mile.

Sabin by itself. Changing only Sabin, and keeping every other line of the recommendation, takes the worst case from 115 to 85. West of NE 15th goes to King, the northern part of the rest to Vernon, and the remainder to Alameda. That is the "Rec + Sabin split" card above. One neighborhood is a real fix, but the rest of the 1,216 need the same kind of fix elsewhere.

How to read this

  • Model estimates, not PPS figures. Kids are the 2031–32 Portland State University (PSU) K–5 forecast, spread across 2020 Census blocks by our model. Walks are street-network walking distances from each block to each school. These are our model's numbers, not PPS's, and they will not match PPS's own enrollment data exactly.
  • "Open school" means a school with a K–5 neighborhood area in that plan. It must be reachable without a new crossing of the Willamette or I-84: a block can count a school across one of them only if the block already crosses there today. Immersion-only (DLI) schools are not counted as open for this measure.
  • Flagged means the assigned school's walk is more than the first distance, and the nearest open school is at or under the second. Some flagged kids may be in immersion or other programs; this counts every K–5 kid by where they live.
  • The plans. Today's lines: PPS's Status Quo map (Lent-area kids outside immersion are counted at Marysville, where they go today). The Superintendent's recommendation of November 12, 2026, as we digitized it. Rec + Sabin split: the recommendation with only Sabin changed. West of NE 15th goes to King, the northern part of the rest to Vernon, the remainder to Alameda (whose kids then go on to Beaumont and Grant). Map A: the best example we modeled. Map B: the recommendation's closures and immersion hubs, plus James John into Sitton, with the lines redrawn. Maps A and B are model examples, not proposals.
  • Kids per plan differ slightly because immersion programs are placed differently in each plan.
