Did PPS learn from SEGC?

PPS paid for an outside audit of its last big boundary change, the 2020–23 Southeast Enrollment and Program Balancing (“SEGC”). Here is what the audit recommended, and how PPS A and PPS B measure up so far.

In 2026, Dr. Terrance Green and Dr. Havala Hanson audited the 20 schools SEGC changed. They drew on 905 family and 327 staff surveys, five years of student and staff records, and focus groups and interviews (audit pp. 4–5). They found that SEGC evened out middle school sizes but did not stop decline, that its projections ran high, and that staff of color and trust took the biggest hits. The Board wrote several of the process lessons into Resolution 7307.

The scenarios apply some of them: every region is in scope, the criteria came before the list, and Marysville’s closure is phased so SEGC-moved students don’t move twice. Others haven’t been published yet, and a few go the other way.

How thin is “sustainable”?

PPS counts a school at its goal (360 K-5, 500 middle) as sustainable. The audit’s planning floor adds a five-year decline buffer: about 450 and 600. Schools between the two lines meet PPS’s goal today but have little room if enrollment keeps falling, as it’s forecast to after 2031 (Res. 7307, recital C).

What would close the gap by mid-November

  1. Publish the equity impact analysis for PPS A and B now, including who moves (by race, poverty, special education and language) and the recently impacted schools, as Resolution 7307 requires.
  2. Show low, expected and high enrollment for every receiving school, not one 2031-32 number.
  3. Say what happens below the line. For schools projected at 360–449 (K-5) or 500–599 (middle), state the staffing they keep if they drift under, and whether they’d be on the next list.
  4. Put a number on net savings, after transition, receiving-school and vacant-building costs.
  5. Publish a cumulative-burden map, and explain each second move: Mandarin, Lent, Atkinson, Creston, Bridger.
  6. Fund transition and receiving-school readiness before the vote, or vote in stages so readiness gates each step.
  7. Release the boundaries as GIS data, so families can test alternatives.

Read with care

  • Two findings cut against simple stories. SEGC students’ test scores, attendance and discipline were broadly similar to matched peers’ (audit p. 7). And immersion students in SEGC schools left PPS at about 6% vs 11% for other students (p. 11).
  • Dr. Green writes that the report “should not be read as a justification for school closures” (p. 152).
  • Many audit findings are participant perceptions, and the audit labels them that way. The 450 / 600 / 650 floors are the auditors’ “preliminary planning estimates,” not PPS policy (pp. 144, 146).
  • “Not yet” is as of October 14, 2026. If PPS publishes any of these, the verdicts here should change.
