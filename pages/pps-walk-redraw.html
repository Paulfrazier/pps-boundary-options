How diverse each school would be

Share of each school's neighborhood K–5 students who are kids of color (any race or ethnicity other than non-Hispanic white), from 2020 Census under-18 counts by block. District-wide it is 42.8%. The walk + diversity redraw keeps the same closures, the same I-84 and river rules and the same 360–600 size range as the walk redraw, but when two nearby schools are both a reasonable walk it leans toward the one that brings each school closer to the district mix.

Students are estimated from Census under-18 counts calibrated to 2024–25 enrollment; immersion students are left out because they choose a program site. Walks are routed on OpenStreetMap footpaths. In the redraw, Faubion's far-north blocks move to Woodlawn, which is why Woodlawn's shares drop and Faubion's rise.

Method. Walking distances come from the OpenStreetMap foot router (routing.openstreetmap.de), from each 2020 Census block's center to its 7 nearest schools. Each block's K–5 students are estimated from its under-18 population and calibrated to each school's 2024–25 neighborhood enrollment; immersion programs stay where PPS is moving them. The redraw assigns each block to the shortest-walk school that keeps schools between 360 and 600 students where geography allows. I-84 and the Willamette are treated as hard edges: a block can only be assigned to a school on its own side of both, with one exception that matches PPS: about 117 students live in blocks that today's Buckman, Laurelhurst and Beverly Cleary areas already reach across I-84, and those blocks may stay with a school on the far side. In Scenario A there is one added crossing, by request: about 87 Rose City Park students in the Hollywood blocks just north of I-84 go to Laurelhurst (about a 1.1-mile walk) instead of Beverly Cleary (about 1.3 miles). After that first pass, every school's area is made one connected piece: blocks are switched to the school most of their neighbors use when that adds no more than 0.2 mi to the walk, any piece cut off from its school joins the neighbor with the shortest walk, and border blocks are traded back so school sizes stay where the first pass put them. That cleanup adds about 0.01 mi to the average walk. Boundary shapes are drawn from the blocks each school receives, then smoothed, so redraw edges are accurate to about a block; today's boundaries are PPS's exact lines. Building capacity, safe-route features like crossing guards, and hills aren't modeled.

Walk + diversity redraw. Starts from the Scenario A walk redraw and trades border blocks between neighboring schools when the trade brings both schools' kids-of-color shares closer to the district share (within 8 points counts as on target). A block can only move to a school whose area it already touches, the walk can't grow by more than half a mile beyond the closest allowed school or past 2 miles, and school sizes stay within the same 360–600 range. Areas are then made one connected piece again.

Smoothing. All three redraws then get a final pass that trades border blocks to shorten and straighten boundaries: a block can switch to a neighboring school when that makes the shared boundary shorter or pulls the area closer around its school, as long as it adds no more than 0.4 mi to anyone’s walk, keeps every school in its size range, keeps each area in one piece and, for the diversity version, doesn’t undo its diversity gains. The I-84 crossings described above are left as they are. In the Scenario A walk redraw, the area around Rigler, Scott, Bridger and Abernethy was also redrawn as a group, favoring rounder areas centered on each school while holding every school’s size. Together these passes cut the total length of school boundaries by about 10% in the walk redraw and 16% in the diversity version without changing average walks. Race and ethnicity come from 2020 Census tables P2 and P4 (total minus 18-and-over) by block; they describe who lives in each block, not who enrolls.
