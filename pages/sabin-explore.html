Exploring Sabin options

The recommendation closes Sabin and sends all of it to Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. This page compares that with ways to split Sabin on streets. In every split, King takes Sabin west of NE 15th; the options differ on how the rest is shared among Vernon, Alameda and Beverly Cleary. Everything outside Sabin stays the recommendation's. There is no clear winner: each option trades walks against school sizes and middle and high school effects. Pick an option in the table and the whole page updates.

Options

The recommendation is the reference and loads first. The rest run from the most of Sabin sent to Alameda down to the fewest. Click a row (or tab to it and press Enter) to show that option everywhere on the page.

School sizes are PPS-anchored: each school starts from PPS's own 2027-28 projection under the recommendation (memo staffing table), grows or shrinks by our model's year-to-year change, and then adds or loses the kids the option moves (our estimate). K-5 totals are 2031-32. Red: King over its 584 seats or under 360, Vernon under 360, Alameda over the model's 600 K-5 cap, Jefferson under 1,100. *Jefferson in 2031-32 if the moved Sabin kids' middle and high school follow their new K-5. Every option is scored the same way, by year; only the lines inside Sabin differ.

Map

Hover over (or tap) any dot to see its walk to the nearby schools, its nearest school, and its school under the selected option.

Each dot is a 2020 Census block in today's Sabin, Irvington, Alameda, King or Vernon area (thin outlines). Dot area is the estimated number of K-5 kids there in 2031-32. Walks are routed on the street network, to the open K-5 schools nearby under the recommendation: King, Alameda, Vernon, Beverly Cleary, Boise-Eliot/Humboldt, Scott, Woodlawn and Faubion. Schools outside the map frame are listed as "(off map)". Laurelhurst is left out because it is across I-84, and Rose City Park is not open as a neighborhood K-5 under the recommendation. The dashed lines are the split lines. The other street lines are approximate and only there to help you find your way.
