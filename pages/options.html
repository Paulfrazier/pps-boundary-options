Model v2 estimates for 2031-32 (years as marked), Census blocks scaled to the PSU forecast. Runs (scorecard.json): mapA4, mapB, super_rec, status_quo, sabin_split, ne_plan. Schools at target are neighborhood schools with an attendance area, between PPS’s floor and the building’s capacity; immersion-only hubs and the two small K-8s (Astor, Skyline) aren’t counted. Green marks the best and red the worst of the options (today’s lines aren’t ranked). Worst walks and moves come from the solver’s worst_walks.py and score_principles.py. How we count.

What the rows mean

  • At target: a neighborhood school with at least PPS’s floor (360 K-5, 500 middle, 1,100 high) and no more than its building holds. The strip shows each year from 2027-28 to 2031-32, the years PPS’s Family Guides project; green cells are years when every school is at target.
  • Past a closer school: kids assigned to a school at least 0.25 mi farther than an open school that is closer.
  • Over 1 mi, with a school within 1 mi (or ½ mi): K-5 kids assigned more than a mile from home although an open school they could reach without a new river or I-84 crossing is within a mile (or half a mile). These are the worst walks.
  • Over the bus line: walks past PPS’s bus distance, 1 mi for K-5 and 1.5 mi for middle and high school.
  • Moved out of schools that stay open: K-5 kids moved away from a school that isn’t closing. Kids whose school closes aren’t counted. Our maps move kids at open schools only for a reason: to fill a short school or for a real walk gain.
  • Middle schools feeding one high school: the share of middle schools whose kids all go on to the same high school.
