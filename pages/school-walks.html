Which schools are open

Starts as the Superintendent's recommendation. Check a school to keep it open as a neighborhood school, uncheck to close it. You can also click a school on the map. Your choices use the closest-open-school view, since new boundaries would need a new solve.

Walk deserts, ranked by density

#AroundWalks toKidsKids / sq miNewly over

A desert is a connected patch of blocks over the line that all walk to the same school (“Walks to”: closest open or assigned, as the view is set), within one area, with at least 50 kids. Density = projected K-5 kids per square mile of the patch. "Around" names today's closest school for most of its kids. Click a row to zoom to it.

Assigned by boundaries measures the walk to the K-5 school whose attendance area each home falls in: today's 2025-26 boundaries vs the boundaries in the Superintendent's recommendation (memo of 2026-11-12) as modeled in this project. Closest open school ignores boundaries and measures the walk to the nearest open neighborhood K-5 or K-8. The gap between the two views is the extra walking that comes from where the lines are drawn rather than from which schools close.

Choosing schools: the checkboxes set which buildings run as neighborhood K-5 / K-8 schools. Each home is then measured to the nearest one that is open. Boundaries for a custom set of schools are not drawn, so the assigned view only covers the rec as written. Capacity is not checked: keeping a school open or closing one does not test whether the neighbors can hold the kids.

Areas: NE = north of Burnside and east of N Williams Ave; N = north of Burnside and west of Williams; SE = south of Burnside on the east side; West side = the Lincoln and Ida B. Wells high school clusters. Totals and the table count only the selected area; kids are measured to any school, even across an area line.

Kids are projected 2031-32 PPS K-5 students by home block (the project's 2031-32 "where kids live" estimate), rounded to the nearest 10. Walk is the shortest walking route on the OpenStreetMap street and path network (local OSRM foot routing) from each block's center to the school. Minutes assume 3 mph, so 15 minutes = 0.75 mile. A 5-to-10-year-old usually walks slower than that.

Density: projected K-5 kids per square mile, each block averaged with its neighbors, in five steps (under 100, 100–200, 200–300, 300–450, 450+; each step holds roughly a fifth of the district’s kids). Walk + density draws the walk colors stronger in denser steps; Density map colors each block by its walk status and its density together: newly over the line runs from light red to near-black, over the line today too from light to dark amber, and within the line in greys. A desert's density divides its kids by its land area (block areas from census-block cells clipped to the district), so patches of parks or industrial land score low even when they are large.

The rec's closures, as modeled: Sabin, Irvington, Rose City Park, Beach, Buckman, Creston, Lewis, Marysville, Peninsula, Woodmere and Cesar Chavez close. Rigler becomes a Spanish dual-language hub with no neighborhood area; Lent and Richmond are hubs too.
